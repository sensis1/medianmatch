    Guide

    How MedianMatch works, and how to pay for college.

    Where the numbers come from

    College figures come from the U.S. Department of Education’s College Scorecard, a free public dataset, using its most recent release (downloaded October 2026). MedianMatch covers over 900 four-year colleges, including test-blind schools such as the University of California and California State University campuses.

    How your chance is estimated

    Your SAT (ACT is converted with the official concordance table) and GPA are compared with each college’s middle 50% of enrolled students. That comparison moves the college’s overall admit rate up or down. Above-average numbers help less at very selective schools, where most applicants already have top numbers. The result is shown as a range. Tap “Why this number?” on any college to see what moved it.

    Reach means under 30% or any college that admits fewer than 15% of applicants. Match is 30% to 71%. Safety is 72% or higher. At colleges that don’t use test scores, the estimate uses your GPA and the admit rate.

    GPA

    Federal data has no GPA figures, so a typical GPA is estimated from each college’s test scores, or from its admit rate when it doesn’t use tests, and marked “est.” Each college’s Common Data Set (sections C11–C12) has its real GPA spread.

    Activities

    Rate each activity from Level 1 (national) to Level 4 (involved). Your strongest ones count most. Activities make up about 10% of the estimate at colleges that admit most applicants and up to about 35% at the most selective. Colleges don’t publish activity data, so this part is a rule of thumb.

    Majors

    The major filter uses federal field-of-study data: how many students earn a bachelor’s in each subject and their median pay four years after graduating (one year when four isn’t reported). “Top 25% pay” compares a program with the same major at the other colleges here. Pay also reflects location and the jobs graduates choose.

    Cost, debt and “Cheaper than you think”

    Prices are average net prices (cost minus grants) for five family income groups, for students who received federal aid. Public colleges show the in-state price; outside your home state the out-of-state tuition difference is added and marked as an estimate. “Cheaper than you think” shows colleges where your price is under half the sticker price. Typical debt is the median federal loan amount for students who graduated, with the estimated monthly payment.

    Paying for college

    FAFSA

    The Free Application for Federal Student Aid is how nearly every college and the government decide your aid, including Pell Grants. It’s free. Fill it out every year you’re in college, as early as you can, at studentaid.gov.

    CSS Profile

    Many private colleges also use the CSS Profile to award their own grants. Check each college’s financial aid page to see if it’s required. Students from lower-income families can often get it for free. It’s run by the College Board at cssprofile.collegeboard.org.

    Application fee waivers

    If application fees are a problem, you can usually get them waived. The Common App and most colleges accept a fee waiver request on the application itself, and your school counselor can confirm you qualify. SAT and ACT fee waivers also come with free score sends and college application fee waivers at many schools.

    QuestBridge

    QuestBridge connects high-achieving students from low-income families with partner colleges, many of which offer full scholarships through its National College Match. If your family income is low and your grades are strong, look into it early in your senior year at questbridge.org.

    Net Price Calculators

    Every college has a Net Price Calculator on its website. It uses your family’s real finances to estimate your price more exactly than averages can. Each college page here links straight to its calculator.

    Privacy and security

    All matching runs in your browser. Scores, GPA, income and activities are never sent anywhere. A small server counts anonymous visits and clicks; the Privacy Policy lists exactly what. There are no accounts, cookies or ads.

    Guía

    Cómo funciona MedianMatch y cómo pagar la universidad.

    De dónde vienen los números

    Los datos de las universidades vienen del College Scorecard del Departamento de Educación de EE. UU., una base de datos pública y gratuita, en su versión más reciente (descargada en octubre de 2026). MedianMatch incluye más de 900 universidades de cuatro años, incluidas las que no usan exámenes, como los campus de la Universidad de California y de la Universidad Estatal de California.

    Cómo se calcula tu probabilidad

    Tu SAT (el ACT se convierte con la tabla oficial) y tu GPA se comparan con el 50% central de los estudiantes inscritos en cada universidad. Esa comparación sube o baja la tasa general de admisión. Los números altos ayudan menos en las universidades muy selectivas, donde casi todos los solicitantes ya tienen números altos. El resultado se muestra como un rango. Toca “¿Por qué este número?” en cualquier universidad para ver qué lo movió.

    Difícil (Reach) significa menos de 30% o cualquier universidad que admite a menos del 15%. Posible (Match) es de 30% a 71%. Segura (Safety) es 72% o más. En las universidades que no usan exámenes, el cálculo usa tu GPA y la tasa de admisión.

    GPA

    Los datos federales no incluyen GPA, así que el GPA típico se estima con los puntajes de cada universidad, o con su tasa de admisión si no usa exámenes, y se marca como “est.” El Common Data Set de cada universidad (secciones C11–C12) tiene sus datos reales.

    Actividades

    Califica cada actividad del Nivel 1 (nacional) al Nivel 4 (participación). Las más fuertes cuentan más. Las actividades pesan cerca del 10% en universidades que admiten a la mayoría y hasta cerca del 35% en las más selectivas. Las universidades no publican datos de actividades, así que esta parte es una regla aproximada.

    Carreras

    El filtro de carreras usa datos federales por área de estudio: cuántos estudiantes se gradúan en cada carrera y su salario medio cuatro años después (un año cuando no hay dato de cuatro). “25% mejor pagado” compara el programa con la misma carrera en las otras universidades. El salario también depende del lugar y de los trabajos que eligen los graduados.

    Costo, deuda y “Más barata de lo que crees”

    Los precios son el precio neto promedio (costo menos becas) para cinco niveles de ingreso familiar, de estudiantes que recibieron ayuda federal. Las universidades públicas muestran el precio para residentes del estado; fuera de tu estado se suma la diferencia de matrícula y se marca como estimado. “Más barata de lo que crees” muestra universidades donde tu precio es menos de la mitad del precio de lista. La deuda típica es el préstamo federal medio de quienes se graduaron, con el pago mensual estimado.

    Cómo pagar la universidad

    FAFSA

    La Solicitud Gratuita de Ayuda Federal para Estudiantes (FAFSA) es como casi todas las universidades y el gobierno deciden tu ayuda, incluida la Beca Pell. Es gratis. Llénala cada año, lo antes posible, en studentaid.gov.

    CSS Profile

    Muchas universidades privadas también usan el CSS Profile para dar sus propias becas. Revisa la página de ayuda financiera de cada universidad. Las familias de menores ingresos muchas veces lo pueden enviar gratis. Lo administra el College Board en cssprofile.collegeboard.org.

    Exención de cuotas de solicitud

    Si las cuotas de solicitud son un problema, normalmente puedes pedir que no te las cobren. El Common App y la mayoría de las universidades aceptan la solicitud de exención en la misma aplicación, y tu consejero escolar puede confirmar que calificas. Las exenciones del SAT y ACT también incluyen envíos de puntajes gratis y exenciones de solicitud en muchas universidades.

    QuestBridge

    QuestBridge conecta a estudiantes destacados de familias de bajos ingresos con universidades asociadas, muchas de las cuales ofrecen becas completas a través de su National College Match. Si el ingreso de tu familia es bajo y tus calificaciones son altas, infórmate al comienzo de tu último año en questbridge.org.

    Calculadoras de precio neto

    Cada universidad tiene una calculadora de precio neto en su sitio web. Usa las finanzas reales de tu familia para estimar tu precio con más precisión que los promedios. Cada página de universidad aquí tiene un enlace directo a su calculadora.

    Privacidad y seguridad

    Todo el cálculo se hace en tu navegador. Tus puntajes, GPA, ingresos y actividades nunca se envían a ningún lugar. Un pequeño servidor cuenta visitas y clics de forma anónima; la Política de Privacidad explica exactamente qué. No hay cuentas, cookies ni anuncios.

    About MedianMatch

    MedianMatch was built by a first-generation college applicant who wanted one honest place to answer two questions: can I get in, and can my family afford it?

    When you’re the first in your family to apply, there’s no one at home who has done this before. Most students end up guessing which schools are realistic, and many skip great colleges because the sticker price looks impossible, even when grants would cover most of it.

    MedianMatch puts the answers in one place using public data from the U.S. Department of Education: your estimated chance as an honest range, what your family would likely pay, typical debt, and how graduates in your major do after college.

    What we promise

    • It’s free, with no accounts and no ads.
    • Your scores, GPA, income and activities never leave your device.
    • Chances are never inflated to make you feel good. They’re estimates, shown as ranges, with the reasons behind them.

    How it was built

    MedianMatch was designed, tested and launched by a high school student, using an AI coding assistant to help write the code. The code is open source on GitHub, where you can also report a problem or suggest a feature.

    Short version: what you type about yourself (scores, GPA, income, activities) never leaves your browser. We count anonymous visits and clicks to improve the site. We don’t use cookies, don’t sell data and don’t show ads.

    What stays in your browser

    Test scores, GPA, activities, household income, home state and price limits are processed only inside your web browser to compute matches and chance estimates. They are never sent to us or to anyone else and never written to a database. Unless you choose to remember your profile (below), closing or reloading the page erases them.

    A few things are remembered in your browser’s local storage, on your device only: the colleges you save to My List, any application status and deadline you add to them, your light or dark mode choice, your language choice, and notes that you agreed to these terms and finished Quick setup and the tour. If you turn on “Remember my profile on this device,” your scores, GPA, income, home state and activities are also kept there so you don’t have to re-enter them; turn it off or use “Clear everything” to erase them. None of this is ever sent to us. Clearing your browser’s site data removes all of it.

    On the website, a small offline helper (a “service worker”) keeps a copy of the page itself in your browser so it loads faster and works without a connection. It stores only the site’s own files, never anything you type.

    Share links

    When you use “Share link” in My List, the link contains only short codes for the colleges on your list. It never contains your scores, GPA, income or activities. We don’t see who you share it with, except that opening a link is counted as an anonymous page visit like any other.

    How visitors are counted

    To count unique visitors without identifying anyone, our server combines your IP address and browser type with a secret random key that is replaced every day, and stores only a scrambled code made from them. The IP address itself is never stored. Because the key changes daily and is never saved, the code can’t be traced back to you or linked across days.
