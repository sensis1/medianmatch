    How MedianMatch works

    Plain-language explanation of the math, so you can judge the results yourself.

    1. Where the numbers come from

    All college figures come from the U.S. Department of Education’s College Scorecard, a free public dataset. MedianMatch includes the roughly 900 operating, primarily bachelor’s-degree colleges that report SAT scores. Colleges that are test-blind (for example, the University of California campuses) don’t report SAT ranges, so they are not in the list.

    2. Test scores

    Each college reports the 25th and 75th percentile SAT scores of enrolled students. That range is the shaded band on each card, and the dot is you. ACT scores are converted to the SAT scale with the official 2018 ACT/College Board concordance table.

    3. GPA

    The federal data has no GPA information. MedianMatch estimates a typical unweighted GPA for each school from its SAT midpoint (higher-scoring schools tend to enroll students with higher GPAs). Every card labels this as an estimate. Check a school’s Common Data Set (section C11–C12) for its real GPA distribution.

    4. The fit score and labels

    Your SAT is measured against each school’s middle 50%, and your GPA against the estimated typical GPA. These combine into one score (60% test, 40% GPA; 100% GPA if you choose test-optional). Then:

    • Reach: your profile is below the school’s typical range, or the school admits fewer than 15% of applicants. Those schools are a reach for everyone.
    • Safety: your profile is clearly above the typical range and the school admits at least 45% of applicants.
    • Match: everything in between.

    “Closest to your profile” sorts schools whose middle students look most like you to the top.

    4b. Extracurricular impact

    Many colleges read applications holistically, so activities can move the needle, and they matter more the more selective a school is. You can rate each activity on four levels: Level 1 (national), Level 2 (state or regional), Level 3 (school leader) and Level 4 (involved). Your strongest activities count most; each extra activity adds a bit less.

    MedianMatch compares your activity strength with what is typical for each school’s selectivity. Activities count for about 10% of the fit score at schools that admit most applicants and up to about 35% at the most selective schools. When your activities change a school’s label, the card tells you. Colleges don’t publish activity data, so this part is a rule of thumb based on how holistic admissions generally work, not a measured statistic.

    4c. Ivy League and Top 20 filters

    “Ivy League” shows the eight Ivy League universities. “Top 20 most selective” and “Top 50 most selective” rank colleges by their published admission rate in the federal data (lowest rate = #1). This is a selectivity ranking, not a quality ranking like U.S. News, and it only includes colleges in MedianMatch.

    5. Cost and aid

    College Scorecard reports the average net price (cost of attendance minus grants and scholarships) for five family income groups: $0–30k, $30–48k, $48–75k, $75–110k and $110k+. MedianMatch places you in your group and shows that number. For public colleges this is the in-state figure; if the college is outside your home state, MedianMatch adds the published out-of-state tuition difference and marks the result as an estimate. A negative or zero net price means grants typically cover the full cost for that group.

    6. Security and privacy design

    MedianMatch has no server, database, account system or analytics. Every calculation runs in your browser and nothing you enter is sent anywhere or saved. With no stored user data and no login, there is nothing on our side to breach. College names and figures are displayed as plain text, so data can’t inject code into the page.
